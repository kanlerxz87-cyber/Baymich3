Five things that matter for a decision

  1. Concentration beats breadth. 25 of 41 Core starred restaurants are inside San Francisco. If you want maximum Michelin with minimum travel, base yourself in the city and do not split the trip.
  2. Wine Country is a second trip, not a day trip. 8 starred restaurants sit in Napa/Sonoma, including three of the seven 3-star rooms. Two of them (Auberge du Soleil, SingleThread) also have rooms, which removes the driving problem.
  3. The 2026 edition moved. Californios went to 3 stars (the first Mexican restaurant in the world to reach that tier) and Enclos was promoted to 3. Naides and Wolfsbane arrived with 1 star. Any list built from a 2025 source is out of date.
  4. Service charges, not menu prices, decide the bill. Published per-person prices run $60 (Angler lunch) to $425 (Benu), but San Francisco adds 20–26% on top, and 7 Adams adds a 6% mandate on top of its 20% service. Two adults at Benu is $1,037 before tax and wine.
  5. The data is deliberately incomplete. 37 of 44 starred rows carry a verified value; 1 are partial and 6 unverified. Prices and hours live behind booking platforms for most venues, so they are blank rather than guessed.

What changed in this audit

  • Data corruption fixed. Eleven rows in the starred table had the Michelin URL sitting in the price column, which had pushed every later field out of place and rendered junk prices on the live site. All 44 rows were rebuilt in canonical column order.
  • Price arithmetic fixed. Acquerello showed $708 against a $731.60 basis, Angler $156 against $151.20, Commis $669 against $669.60, and 7 Adams omitted its 6% mandate entirely.
  • Hotel addresses corrected. Three were wrong, including one that listed The French Laundry's address as a hotel's.
  • Provenance corrected. Bib rows outside San Francisco had been citing the San Francisco list as their source.
  • Cluster count corrected. Wine Country has 8 Core starred restaurants, not 7 — the earlier text said 7 while listing 8 names.
